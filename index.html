Abstract

Many borrowers do not refinance even when doing so would lower their payments. We show that this is partly driven by a lender-side friction: the difficulty of valuing a house, or collateral uncertainty, restricts refinancing. Using loan performance and property records, we find that borrowers refinance less where homes are harder to value, even when refinancing would yield net savings. Mortgage application data show that greater uncertainty about local home values predicts a higher likelihood that lenders turn down a refinance application. In these markets, refinance appraisals rely on fewer and more distant comparable sales, making valuations less precise. Foreclosed homes also sell at larger discounts, pointing to weaker recovery in default. When mortgage rates fall, borrowers are less likely to refinance where homes are harder to value, leaving substantial savings unrealized.
